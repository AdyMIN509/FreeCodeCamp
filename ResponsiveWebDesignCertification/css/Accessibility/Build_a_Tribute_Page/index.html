Dr. Norman Borlaug

The man who save a billion lives

Dr. Norman Borlaug, third from the left, trains biologist in Mexico on how to increase wheat yields - part of his life-long war on hunger

Here's a time line of Dr. Borlaug's life:

  • 1914: Born March 25 near Cresco, Iowa, to a Norwegian-American farming family.
  • 1933: Enrolls at the University of Minnesota (initially struggles to gain admission, gets in through a general college program).
  • 1937: Earns a B.S. in Forestry from the University of Minnesota.
  • 1942: Earns a Ph.D. in Plant Pathology from the University of Minnesota.
  • 1942–1944: Works as a microbiologist at DuPont.
  • 1944: Joins the Rockefeller Foundation's cooperative agricultural program in Mexico to help improve wheat production.
  • 1940s–1950s: Develops high-yield, disease-resistant wheat varieties in Mexico, including semi-dwarf varieties that resist lodging (falling over) under heavy grain loads.
  • 1960s: His wheat varieties are introduced to India and Pakistan, dramatically increasing yields and helping avert famine — a key moment in what becomes known as the "Green Revolution."
  • 1970: Awarded the Nobel Peace Prize for his contributions to the world food supply.
  • 1984: Joins Texas A&M University as a distinguished professor.
  • 1986: Founds the World Food Prize to recognize achievements in improving global food security.
  • 1990s–2000s: Continues advocacy work, focusing on food security in Africa through organizations like the Sasakawa Africa Association.
  • 2009: Dies on September 12 in Dallas, Texas, at age 95.
